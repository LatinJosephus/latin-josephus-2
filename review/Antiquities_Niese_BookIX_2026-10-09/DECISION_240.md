# IX.239–240: exact cut on a shared printed line

Status: CLOSED — A adopted by explicit user editorial resolution on 2026-10-09. This resolves the word-level ambiguity of the shared-line numeral; it does not claim that the printed numeral unambiguously fixes the cut at ἔσται.

Niese II (1885), printed 317, PDF 325 places marginal 240 on the first narrative line: `ἀργύριον ἁρπάσατε.” ἔσται δ᾽ οὐδεὶς βουλησόμενος· σώζειν γὰρ`. The numeral identifies that line, but is not an inline word separator. [Full printed page](evidence/Niese-pdf-325.png).

Loeb VI (Marcus, 1958), Greek printed 126, PDF 142 places marginal 240 against the line containing the end of `ἁρπάσατε`, then `ἔσται δ᾽ οὐδεὶς βουλησόμενος· σώζειν γὰρ`. This independently supports the same line without fixing the exact word. [Control image](evidence/Loeb-control-142.png).

Adjoining Greek: `… στῆτε καὶ μείνατε καὶ χρυσὸν αὑτοῖς καὶ ἄργυρον ἁρπάσατε. ἔσται δ᾽ οὐδεὶς βουλησόμενος· σώζειν γὰρ αὑτῶν ἐθελήσουσι τὰς ψυχὰς μᾶλλον ἢ τὰ κτήματα …` — “Stand and remain, and seize gold and silver for yourselves. But no one will be willing: they will prefer to save their lives rather than their possessions …”

Adjoining Latin, paragraph `latin-book09-num239`: `… state et manete aurumque uobis argentumque diripiente, et nullus hanc uoluntatem habebit dum animas suas omnes magis quam facultates saluare festinent. Crudelis enim …` — “Stand and remain, and seize gold and silver for yourselves; and no one will have this willingness, since all hasten to save their lives rather than their possessions …”

**A, adopted by explicit user resolution:** move the Greek 240 marker before `ἔσται δ᾽ οὐδεὶς βουλησόμενος`; insert Latin 240 before `et nullus hanc uoluntatem habebit`. IX.239 ends after the commands; IX.240 contains the refusal and its explanation together. The shared printed line permits this reading without settling the exact word. The user chose A to keep the statement that nobody will comply together with its explanation that saving their lives takes priority over saving their possessions.

**B, rejected alternative retained in the decision history:** retain the inherited Greek marker before `σώζειν γὰρ`; insert Latin 240 before `dum animas suas`. IX.239 includes the refusal; IX.240 begins its causal explanation, with the Latin subordinate clause depending on IX.239.

Both alternatives preserve every word, punctuation mark and whitespace byte. They change only the extent of IX.239 and the beginning of IX.240; IX.241 onward is unaffected. Neither entails absence or reconstruction. The existing marker alone does not adjudicate between the two defensible line-level readings.
