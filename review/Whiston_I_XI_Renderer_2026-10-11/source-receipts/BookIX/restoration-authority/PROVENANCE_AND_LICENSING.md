# Documentary provenance and reuse assessment

## Elected restoration witness

The restored region follows William Whiston's translation as printed in *The Works of Flavius Josephus*, Auburn and Rochester: Alden & Beardsley, **1856**, printed pages **251–255**, University of Toronto / Kelly Library copy. The title leaf was visually read. [The scan catalogue](https://archive.org/details/worksofflaviusj00joseuoft) identifies the publisher, date, contributor and NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT assessment. The complete preserved scan has SHA-256 `2753e37ffc9567d4f10a079bc7f7e11d10386fa16c31167503a89c34a038e912`, 71,866,929 bytes and 900 PDF pages. PDF pages 263–267 correspond to the five printed pages; the title is PDF page 11. These locators apply to this frozen PDF, not necessarily a regenerated Archive PDF. Full page images, enlarged details and the catalogue metadata are included for offline recovery. The original 68.5 MiB PDF remains at `C:/workspace/LatinJosephus-Whiston-Contents-Audit-20261008/sources/Whiston1856.pdf`; it is not required for offline verification of the recovery ZIP, which holds the necessary page images.

This is not a transcription of the 1737 first edition. Whiston first published the translation in 1737; the elected physical witness is 1856. The project previously elected this printing for its compiled Whiston chapter-heading indexes, which supports its editorial compatibility without proving that the original narrative was transcribed from the same copy.

## Digital controls and dependence

The three requested [Lexundria ranges](https://lexundria.com/j_aj/9.45-9.94/wst), [95–104](https://lexundria.com/j_aj/9.95-9.104/wst) and [105–139](https://lexundria.com/j_aj/9.105-9.139/wst) supply explicit published-HTML Niese spans. Their edition overview names the 1737 first publication but does not identify the transcribed later edition, digital ancestor or a resource-specific reuse licence. The home page and overview were inspected; no such grant was found there. No contact email was sent.

The [Perseus TEI](https://github.com/PerseusDL/canonical-greekLit/blob/master/data/tlg0526/tlg001/tlg0526.tlg001.perseus-eng2.xml) explicitly identifies Alden & Beardsley 1856, credits the Perseus Project / Trustees of Tufts University, Gregory Crane, Lisa Cerrato and David Smith, and links the same historical scan. Its revision history begins in 1997 and includes later quotation-mark changes. Its divisions begin the eight missing Whiston paragraphs at 51, 60, 74, 87, 95, 99, 102 and 105; this is coarse segmentation, not every Niese boundary.

Lexundria and Perseus share distinctive digital defects here: four **Berthadad** readings, **lm** for **he**, **Ramorb** for **Ramoth**, and **dore's** for printed **dove's**. The controls differ at **ahunting / a hunting** and **he / be**, among spacing and quotation details. Their near-identical wording and shared defects strongly suggest a common digital lineage; direct descent in a particular direction is **not proved** by the inspected metadata. They are therefore treated as dependent controls, never as two independent votes. Gutenberg also shares **dore's**, while avoiding several of the other errors. The printed page images adjudicate the shared defects; OCR merely assists searching.

## Editorial representation

Eight Whiston paragraphs (IV.3–6, V.1–3 and VI.1), seven historical notes and the V/VI chapter headings are restored. Paragraph IV.3 retains the original `english-book09-num51` ID and sameAs. Its inherited pointer still leads to the Latin lacuna; it does not assert that Latin text has been recovered. Seven new paragraph IDs, two heading IDs and seven note IDs are confined to the repair. Existing book/chapter wrappers are preserved; no new Niese numbers or milestones are inserted. Whiston paragraph numbers use p/@n and the recovery ledger. Heading paragraphs use p/@rend, following the repository's paragraph-based paratext approach; later alignment must omit these headings from the narrative matching stream. Historical notes remain separate note elements, correctly anchored, with page-local asterisk/dagger symbols retained in @n. The translational narrative and apparatus can be extracted separately.

Printed quotation boundaries, spelling and punctuation are represented, with straight ASCII apostrophes and double quotation marks to follow canonical encoding practice. Physical line wraps, running heads, page furniture and line-end word divisions are not narrative text. Lexical hyphens and dashes remain; the note's numerical range uses an en dash. Font changes, small capitals, italic type and page layout are not reproduced as a diplomatic facsimile. The full page images preserve them. PRINT_DECISIONS.json records every elected change from the Gutenberg drafting scaffold, and the complete character collation records every difference against all three digital controls. The retained footnote about defects in Josephus's copies is Whiston's historical apparatus, **not** a claim that the project's 59-section English lacuna reflects an omission by Whiston.

## Rights and incorporation

The 1856 narrative and historical apparatus, by a translator who died in 1752, are historical public-domain material. The scan catalogue's copyright assessment is evidence for that particular item; it is not a licence for modern editorial contributions. [Canada's Copyright Act, s. 6](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-42/page-1.html) supplies the general author-life-plus-70 term. [17 USC 103(b)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title17-section103&num=0&edition=prelim) distinguishes new contributions from underlying material. These support treating historic wording separately from a modern transcription's protectable additions.

Perseus's file-specific header expressly grants **CC BY-SA 4.0**; the frozen repository README repeats that default and requests offered modifications. The complete unchanged control file retains that notice and attribution. [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode.en) requires credit, identification of changes and ShareAlike for protected adapted material, and excludes uses not requiring copyright permission. [Compatible licences](https://creativecommons.org/compatible-licenses/) must be considered if protected Perseus contributions are incorporated into differently licensed work. Copying its TEI encoding or protected editorial additions into an all-rights-reserved source would not be justified merely by Whiston's age. No such licence compatibility is assumed here, and no message or upstream contribution has been sent without authorization.

Project Gutenberg 2848 is catalogued as [public domain in the USA](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2848), produced by David Reed and David Widger. Its [licence](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/license.html) separates underlying unrestricted book text from trademark/packaging conditions. The archived control retains its original licence; this candidate is not branded as a Gutenberg ebook. Gutenberg supplied a drafting scaffold, corrected against the elected printed pages. Modern control metadata, page layout, HTML, TEI markup and uncertain digital errors are not imported into the canonical candidate. New XML is independently authored using the project's conventions. The restored wording and historical notes are accountable to the identified 1856 print.

**Reuse assessment: the narrow candidate is suitable for subsequent segmentation.** A later incorporation should preserve this source attribution and the distinct notices governing the included control files. The research archive is a provenance package, not a blanket public relicensing of Lexundria HTML, Perseus TEI, the original project's XML or other witnesses. An explicit global English licensing framework was not recovered from the inspected canonical metadata/about page; no new licence or copyright declaration is imposed on pre-existing project content. Publication of the entire research ZIP or a blanket relicensing of the canonical English is not recommended on this evidence. This does not prevent incorporation of the print-supported historical restoration; that choice adds no assumed Lexundria permission or imported protected Perseus edition markup. No publication, merger or licence-policy edit occurs in this task.
