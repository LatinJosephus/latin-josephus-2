# XV.283–284: printed line placement and independent control

Niese III (1892), printed 382–383, PDF454–455, was visually inspected. The marginal 283 stands beside the end of the hidden-weapons construction and the blind man's introduction follows on the same line. The marginal 284 shares the first line of printed 383 with the end of the preceding clause, the inline older division 4, and `ταῦτα γνόντες`. The numeral itself is not a word tag.

Loeb VIII, Greek printed 134/PDF150, independently places 283 beside the hidden-weapons line and its following `ἦν δ᾽` clause. It places 284 at the separate line beginning `(4) Ταῦτα γνόντες`. The adjoining English printed 135/PDF151 was also inspected for context. Greek printed 132/PDF148 supplies the preceding 277–281 context. The exact adopted Greek incipits are the existing XML `ἦν δ᾽` for 283 and `ταῦτα γνόντες` for 284. No Greek marker or wording is changed. The independent control and complete clause context support these incipits; the inline older heading alone is not their authority. The frozen structural record's earlier ambiguity about citation containment remains unchanged and is distinguished from this new section-level review.

Latin 283 begins `inter quos fuit quidam`, retaining the blind man's contribution of encouragement. Latin 284 begins `Quod consilium cum deliberassent`; the plan to kill others if the king escaped is shortened. Both sections have surviving correspondence. The physical cuts are routine source-supported placements, with compression recorded separately.
