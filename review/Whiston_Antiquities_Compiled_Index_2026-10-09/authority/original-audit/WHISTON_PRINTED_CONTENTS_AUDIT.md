# Printed contents: scope and limits

The title-page word “Contents” does not itself establish a discrete list: it might describe chapter summaries in situ or another apparatus. Modern website menus are not treated as printed lists.

The 1856 frontmatter has library/blank leaves, portrait/title and the Life opening. Antiquities has its own Preface followed by Book I’s interval heading, Chapter I and narrative. Every other Antiquities opening proceeds from book/interval text to Chapter I and narrative. No book-level summary list was observed at those openings. The final Antiquities passage is followed directly by the Wars title/Preface. Backmatter has the Hades extract, dissertations, reference tables, alphabetical index and advertisements. The middle alphabetical index pages, PDF 871–890, were located through OCR/scandata but not visually read; the opening and closing index pages were inspected. This does not claim visual inspection of every page of every work in the volume.

The 1741 reader’s Google “Table of Contents” menu is interface navigation: its PP9 destination is the title page. Gutenberg’s HTML links and Chicago’s online book index likewise establish digital arrangements, not their existence in 1737.

A complete 1737 page-image set is still needed to settle the original advertised “Contents”: all preliminary leaves, the Antiquities and individual book openings, and the 83 final unnumbered pages need examination. Accessible additional 1741 volumes or another complete early printing could support an earlier governing witness after separate edition-specific collation. Editions must not be mixed silently.

Recommendation: an editorially compiled heading index, subject to approval of its stated 1856 authority and the two Book III qualifications. Do not call it “Whiston’s 1737 table of contents”. If an early printed list is later established, reproduce it as a separately identified witness without rewriting this audit’s history.
