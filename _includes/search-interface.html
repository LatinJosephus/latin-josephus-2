Text and scope

Currently indexed: Cardwell’s Latin Bellum Judaicum, all seven books including the proem of Book I; Ussani’s Latin De excidio, all five books and the Prologue; Boysen’s Latin Contra Apionem, both books; and the Bamberg Msc. Class. 78 Latin Antiquities, Books I–XX with the separate Proem included in Book I. Whiston’s English Antiquities covers the Proem and Books I–XX, a total of 7,376 Niese sections. Explicitly marked titles, citation labels and annotation notes are excluded. Source additions and untagged letters within the accepted passages remain searchable, including inline Roman chapter letters.

Every word in the indexed main text remains searchable, including common words such as the and and. Spelling is preserved in the excerpts; the optional u/v and i/j equivalence setting affects Latin matching only. English keeps its source spelling apart from capitalization. No grammatical stemming or automatic spelling correction is applied.

Within each citation unit: every Boolean condition and exact phrase is evaluated in that unit. Bellum, Contra Apionem and Antiquities use Niese sections; De excidio uses the reader’s book, chapter and paragraph citations, with Prol. for the Prologue.

Whole book: Boolean conditions may be satisfied anywhere in the same book; exact phrases must still be consecutive and may cross citation or chapter boundaries. Each book, work and edition is evaluated separately. A phrase never continues into another book, from the De excidio Prologue into its Book I narrative, or from the separate Antiquities Proem into its Book I narrative.

Passages: results follow the order of the text. A citation unit with several matches appears once in citation-unit scope. In whole-book scope, a phrase spanning units can have its own excerpt. Click a citation to open the corresponding passage in the reader.

Concordance: each positive word or phrase match has its own row, with up to ten words of context on each side. Original spelling is retained. A phrase spanning citation units appears in one row.

Editorial text: in De excidio, [brackets] mark supplied letters, struck text marks deletion, and † indicates textual uncertainty. Supplied and deleted letters remain searchable, including letters inside a word. A dagger is treated as punctuation, so phrase searches may match across it. The CSV retains exact text and offsets and adds editorial columns using [supplied] and ⟦deleted⟧.

Contra Apionem markings: the source’s asterisks, brackets, angle brackets and Greek quotations are retained. Exact phrases stop at asterisk-marked interruptions, including those at section boundaries; Boolean conditions can relate words on either side. Bold green text marks an addition encoded in the source, shown as ⟨added text⟩ in the CSV’s editorial columns. The search makes no new textual repairs.

Bamberg Latin Antiquities markings and coverage: added, deleted and uncertain letters remain searchable, displayed as bold green text, struck text and dotted underlining. Original brackets, ellipses and erased-dot interruptions are preserved. Exact phrases stop at marked gaps, empty source intervals, the documented Book IX lacuna and the delimited interruptions between XVI.188 and 200 and between XX.26 and 37; Boolean conditions can relate words on either side. III.240 and VII.69–99 retain their citations and encoded omissions. X.108 has a citation label but no Latin before the next Niese marker. IX.51–109 are absent from the accepted source and have no generated records. Other identities marked unavailable in the accepted maps also have no generated records; a skipped number alone does not imply a physical gap. Split portions in Books XI and XVI share Boolean scope, while exact phrases follow physical source order. The two Bellum insertions in XI retain separate citations. The CSV preserves these distinctions, source markings and coverage metadata. Proem results link directly to the separate Proem reader.

Whiston English Antiquities: the Proem and Books I–XX retain their original English spelling, punctuation and Niese section divisions. Book IX includes sections 51–109 from the accepted English source. Headings, printed citation labels and footnotes are excluded. Exact phrases follow physical source order, including the displaced sequence in Book XI. In whole-book scope they may cross physically adjacent Niese sections, but stay within one book and never cross from the Proem into the narrative. English and Latin editions are searched separately, even when both are selected; Boolean conditions and phrases cannot combine evidence from different editions. Proem results link to the separate Proem reader with Whiston selected.

Counts: a single word, wildcard or quoted phrase has an occurrence count as well as a count of matching citation units or books. Boolean queries count matching units or books; concordance rows show their positive matches and are not word frequencies. A citation unit or book can satisfy a negative condition without a word to highlight.

Download all results: the CSV includes every concordance row and the citations of any matching sections or books without positive matches, regardless of which view or page is displayed. It records the completed query, its settings, the edition and links to the reader. Changes typed into the search box take effect after you press Search.
